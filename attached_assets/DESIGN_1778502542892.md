## Overview
Exon should feel like a manufacturing control room rendered as clean software rather than a marketing site. The UI is sober, practical, and high-contrast without becoming harsh. It favors white work surfaces, charcoal text, thin zinc borders, and quiet structure over decoration. The goal is operational confidence: users should feel that numbers, statuses, and workflow state are reliable and immediately readable.

The interface is light-first. A dark palette exists as a secondary token set, but the primary identity is a bright canvas with restrained neutral layering. Visual emphasis comes from hierarchy, density control, and stateful surfaces, not from gradients or saturated brand color. The only strong chroma is reserved for destructive and error signaling plus chart accents.

Copy is functional and direct. Most UI text is in Brazilian Portuguese, so labels should stay short, task-oriented, and explicit. The product voice should sound like a serious internal tool for factory operations, inventory, and production planning.

## Colors
The color system is effectively a zinc-neutral semantic palette.

- **Background / Card / Popover:** pure white surfaces keep forms, tables, and dialogs crisp.
- **Foreground:** near-black text provides the main sense of authority and legibility.
- **Secondary / Muted / Accent:** soft near-white grays create hover states, inactive fills, sidebar highlights, and secondary containers without introducing a separate brand hue.
- **Border / Input / Ring:** low-chroma grays define structure and focus with minimal noise.
- **Destructive:** a vivid red is reserved for validation, deletion, rejection, and waste states. It should always read as urgent and isolated from normal navigation.
- **Charts:** charts may use the provided orange, teal, deep blue, yellow, and amber sequence, but charts should still sit inside the neutral shell of the product rather than turning the whole page colorful.

The visual personality depends on discipline. Do not invent additional accent colors for buttons, navigation, or empty states. In normal operation, the UI should remain mostly white, charcoal, and zinc.

## Typography
Typography is utilitarian and modern. Use the default system sans stack for almost everything. This keeps the product familiar, fast, and neutral across platforms. Hierarchy comes from weight, scale, and spacing rather than from expressive font pairing.

- **Page titles and major card titles:** semi-bold, 24px equivalent, tight tracking.
- **Body copy:** regular 16px or 14px depending on density needs.
- **Buttons, labels, table headers:** medium weight at 14px.
- **Badges and fine metadata:** 12px semi-bold.
- **SKUs, lot numbers, operation sequences, and other coded values:** monospace at small sizes.

Monospace use is important. Structured identifiers should visibly separate themselves from prose so operators can scan tables quickly and distinguish human-facing names from system-facing codes.

## Layout & Spacing
Spacing follows a 4px base unit but most real compositions land on 8px, 12px, 16px, and 24px steps. The product is compact, though not cramped. It behaves like an operations dashboard: enough air to read comfortably, not enough air to feel editorial or leisurely.

- **Global app shell:** fixed left sidebar and scrollable content area.
- **Page rhythm:** most pages use 16px or 24px vertical stacks.
- **Cards and dialogs:** 24px internal padding.
- **Forms:** 8px between label and field, 16px to 24px between field groups.
- **Tables:** 16px cell padding with consistent row height.
- **Content widths:** forms stay constrained; data views can spread wider but should still feel containerized.

Authentication pages are slightly softer than the rest of the product. They place a centered card on a muted background, but they should still feel like the same system, not a separate marketing funnel.

## Elevation & Depth
Depth is restrained. Surfaces are primarily differentiated by border, fill, and small shadows.

- **Standard cards:** light shadow only, just enough to detach white cards from the page background.
- **Select menus and popovers:** medium shadow.
- **Dialogs:** dark scrim plus large but still controlled shadow.

There should be no glossy layering, dramatic blur, or floating-canvas aesthetics. Exon is planar and stable. Depth exists to explain interaction priority, not to create spectacle.

## Shapes
Shape language is soft-industrial: mostly rectangles with moderate rounding. The base radius is 10px, producing 8px medium corners for inputs and buttons and 10px large corners for cards. Full pills are reserved for badges and avatars.

Do not sharpen corners to zero unless matching a native table or browser primitive, and do not introduce oversized rounded corners that make the interface feel playful. Controls should feel deliberate, sturdy, and production-ready.

## Components
Buttons, badges, inputs, cards, tables, and dialogs all inherit the same visual rules:

- **Primary buttons:** dark fill, white text, medium-weight label.
- **Outline buttons:** white surface with input-border framing.
- **Ghost buttons:** transparent until hover, used for secondary actions in dense layouts.
- **Badges:** compact pills used for statuses, types, and operational states.
- **Inputs and selects:** white by default, muted only for read-only display rows.
- **Cards:** white containers with subtle borders and shadow, used heavily for summaries and grouped workflows.
- **Tables:** quiet structure, muted headers, hover tint only, no zebra striping.
- **Dialogs:** centered panels with a dark scrim and tight, focus-first composition.

Navigation should feel like an application shell, not a website menu. Active sidebar items use a muted highlight rather than a bright brand accent. Icons remain small, thin, and functional.

## Do's and Don'ts
- Do keep surfaces neutral and let hierarchy come from typography, spacing, and state.
- Do use monospace for SKUs, lot numbers, and other operational identifiers.
- Do preserve compact enterprise density, especially in forms, tables, and workflow detail pages.
- Do use muted backgrounds for selected, hovered, or read-only states before reaching for color.
- Do keep destructive red isolated to errors, delete actions, and failure states.
- Do keep Portuguese product copy concise and task-specific.

- Do not add gradients, glowing shadows, glass effects, or playful illustration-driven treatments.
- Do not turn primary actions blue, green, or brand-colored; primary action is near-black in the light theme.
- Do not use large hero typography or oversized whitespace that makes operational pages feel like landing pages.
- Do not make tables visually loud with stripes, heavy borders, or too many badge colors.
- Do not mix expressive marketing typography into the application shell.
